What frozen pipe water damage really is

A frozen pipe rarely floods while it is frozen. Ice blocks the line. The flood starts when the ice thaws and water pours through a split in the pipe wall. That is why people often discover frozen pipe damage hours after the temperature rises, and why this page is separate from a general burst pipe.

If your Dunedin home had a hard freeze and now water is in a wall, ceiling or floor, you are in the thaw stage. Stop the water first. Then call this line for extraction and drying.

How often does this happen in Dunedin?

Dunedin is on the Gulf coast of Pinellas County, and freezing temperatures are not a regular part of winter here. No local frequency figure is quoted here because none is published for this page. The National Weather Service Tampa Bay office is the source for current freeze warnings and cold-weather guidance.

When a freeze warning does arrive, damage tends to hit homes that are not built for it. Exposed pipes on a lanai, irrigation lines, pool equipment, outdoor spigots, and supply lines in an attic or an uninsulated garage wall are the usual weak points.

Where frozen pipes usually split

These areas are the first to fail when temperatures drop below freezing.

Outdoor spigots and irrigation

Water trapped in a hose bib or backflow assembly expands and cracks brass or plastic.

Pool equipment lines

Water standing in pumps, heaters and unprotected plumbing can split fittings.

Attic supply lines

Lines in an unconditioned attic can freeze when a cold night sits long enough.

Garage and lanai plumbing

Washer hookups and exterior wall lines in unconditioned spaces are exposed.

A thawed pipe leak is an emergency: what to do first

Close the main water valve first. Then open the lowest faucet to drain the line. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. If water is near a breaker or outlet, stay away and call the utility or 911 for electrical risk before you call us.

Do not use open flames, a torch or a high-heat tool to thaw a pipe. Heat applied unevenly can split a pipe or start a fire. A hair dryer on low or warm towels is safer, and a plumber can thaw a line properly.

After the thaw, the building still needs drying

A split pipe that thawed behaves like any other supply line failure, so the burst pipe page explains the flooding that follows. Water that ran down inside walls often shows up in a ceiling, which the ceiling water damage page covers, and the structural drying page explains how wet framing is dried.

Standards behind this page

Once water is released by a split pipe, the work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for extraction and drying. The standard measures progress by moisture readings, not by how long the equipment has run.
