Commercial water damage is a business problem as well as a building problem

A leak in a home is a household disruption. A leak in a store, office or apartment building stops revenue, displaces tenants and raises liability questions. Commercial water damage restoration has to move quickly and document everything, because the property manager, the owner and the insurer all need records.

This page covers businesses and multi-unit buildings in Dunedin. For a single-family home, start with the page that matches the source, such as burst pipes or water extraction.

When a commercial water loss is an emergency

Call right away if water is moving through ceilings, across a sales floor, into a server closet or through shared walls. The 24/7 line is open at night, on weekends and on holidays, which matters because commercial leaks often start after closing.

Shut off water to the affected area if you can, protect inventory and records, and keep staff and customers out of wet areas. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us.

First steps for a business

These steps limit the loss while a crew is on the way.

Stop the source

Close the shutoff for the fixture or the building main. Tell the property manager if the source is in a shared area.

Protect inventory and records

Lift stock off the floor, cover shelving and move papers and electronics up high.

Keep people safe

Close wet areas to staff and customers. Wet floors are a slip risk.

Document

Photograph and video everything. Keep lists of damaged stock and equipment.

Why records matter on a commercial loss

Commercial claims are won on documentation. Ask for dated photos, the moisture readings from each visit, a list of removed materials and the equipment log. These show the insurer how far the water spread and how long drying took.

Keep a running list of damaged stock, furniture and equipment, with purchase information where you have it. If tenants or neighbors are affected, keep a written record of who was notified and when.

Working with the property manager and your insurer

A commercial loss usually involves more people. A lease can set who is responsible for which repairs. The building's policy and the tenant's policy may each apply. Photos, moisture readings and a written scope help all parties understand what happened.

We explain the scope and price before work starts. If a loss involves a landlord and a tenant, each should read their own lease and policy. Questions about who pays are for the lease, the insurer and a lawyer.

What drives commercial restoration costs

Commercial jobs vary widely.

  • Square footageLarge floor areas need more equipment.
  • Building materialsConcrete, carpet tile, drywall and ceiling tiles respond differently.
  • Water categoryClean water costs less than contaminated water.
  • Access and hoursWorking around business hours is a scheduling cost.
  • Drying timeSet by readings.

Commercial property policies differ from homeowners policies. Sudden water losses are often covered, and slow leaks often are not. Business interruption is a separate coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Standards and references

Commercial water loss follows the same published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, and mold that follows a loss is covered by the S520 standard. Ask any company whether its crews work to them and to show readings.
