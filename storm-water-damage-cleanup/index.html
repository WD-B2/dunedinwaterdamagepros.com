What storm water damage covers

Storm water damage is water that enters through the building shell during a storm. That includes wind-driven rain through damaged shingles, broken windows, failed flashing or a gap around a door. The water arrives from above and from the sides.

This page is about weather that gets through the roof and walls. Rising water from outside is covered on the flood damage cleanup page, and a single roof leak is on the ceiling water damage page.

When storm water is an emergency

If water is entering now or is still pooled inside, treat it as an emergency. Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30, according to the National Hurricane Center, and Tampa Bay sees heavy rain at other times too. Water that sits in insulation and drywall for days leads to larger repairs.

After the storm passes and it is safe, tarp or cover openings if you can, then call. The line is open 24/7, including during a storm recovery.

After the storm

Do these in order, as conditions allow.

Check safety

Stay away from downed power lines and standing water that might be energized.

Cover openings

A roofer or temporary covering protects the building from more rain.

Remove what you can

Move wet contents to a dry area and lift items off wet floors.

Document

Photograph all damage before cleanup for your insurer.

Hidden storm water: attics, wall cavities and insulation

Wind-driven rain does not always leave a puddle. It can enter an attic edge, run down inside a wall and soak insulation, with the only outside sign being a faint stain days later. Fiberglass insulation holds water against drywall and framing, and wet cellulose can sag and stay wet for a long time.

After a storm, look at the ceiling corners, the tops of exterior walls and the area around windows. If you see a stain, bubbling paint or a damp smell, ask for a moisture reading before you repaint. Hidden storm water is the usual path to a mold problem later.

Cleanup and drying after a storm

Crews extract water, remove soaked insulation and drywall that cannot dry, and set air movers and dehumidifiers. Wind-driven rain is usually clean water at first, but if storm surge or drainage water is involved, the contamination steps apply.

Drying is described on the structural drying page, and if drywall or walls need repair the wall and drywall repair page covers it.

What shapes storm water cleanup costs

Storm losses range from small to large.

  • Entry point and spreadOne room versus a whole floor.
  • Water sourceRain versus surge or drainage water.
  • MaterialsInsulation, drywall and flooring.
  • Drying timeBased on readings.
  • RepairsRoof and window repair is separate.

Windstorm and flood are handled differently by insurers. Many Florida policies have separate deductibles for named storms, and flood damage is usually excluded unless you have flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Storm recovery in Dunedin

In October 2024, the City of Dunedin began assessing damaged structures as part of FEMA requirements during storm recovery. The city has since said that building permits were issued to repair or replace most flood-damaged structures.

If your property needs structural repairs after a storm, ask the city building department what permit, if any, applies. The city's Permits and Inspections page lists storm recovery information.
