What a burst pipe does inside a house

A burst pipe is a pressurized line that has split, popped a fitting or pulled out of a joint. Unlike a slow drip, it releases water at full line pressure, which is why a single failure can wet several rooms within the hour. The cleanup starts with stopping the water and ends with a dry structure.

This page covers pressurized supply lines. A line that froze and thawed has its own page on frozen pipe damage, and a failure inside a tank is covered under water heater leaks.

When a burst pipe becomes an emergency

If water is spraying or pouring, treat it as an emergency right now. Go to the main shutoff, which in many Florida homes sits near the street side of the house or at the meter box, and close it. Then open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line.

After hours, a call to this line is the right second step. We answer 24/7 and send extraction and drying crews when they are available for Dunedin. Do not wait for morning if the floor is wet, because wet drywall and subfloor start absorbing from the first minutes.

From shutoff to dry building

Here is the usual order of work on a burst pipe loss.

1

Stop and document

The water is shut off, then photos and video of wet areas are taken before anything is moved. These help with an insurance claim.

2

Extract and open up

Standing water is pulled out. Wet baseboards, saturated insulation or ruined pad may be removed so air can reach what is left.

3

Dry with equipment

Air movers and dehumidifiers run while moisture meters track progress until readings match dry reference materials.

The plumber repairs the pipe. The restoration crew handles the water it released. These are two different jobs, and you may need both.

Copper, PEX and older supply lines

Pipe material affects how and why lines fail. Older galvanized steel corrodes from the inside. Copper can pit or split at joints. PEX tubing resists freezing damage but can fail at fittings or when it is stressed. None of this tells you what failed in your house, but it explains why a plumber will want to inspect more than the single split.

If your home has original supply lines from the decade it was built, ask a licensed plumber about their condition after a burst. Dunedin has older neighborhoods near downtown and the Historic Southside, and a failure in one line can mean other sections have the same age and wear.

What drives the cost of burst pipe cleanup

Prices vary with the loss. These are the factors that set them.

  • Water categoryA clean supply line is Category 1 at the start. Water that sits for days can degrade to Category 2.
  • Rooms and square footageWater travels. The number of rooms and floor area wet sets equipment counts.
  • MaterialsHardwood, carpet and cabinets respond differently to soaking, and some cannot be dried in place.
  • Drying daysEquipment runs until readings come down. That time is priced after moisture mapping.
  • Rebuild scopeRemoved drywall and flooring have to be replaced. That is a separate scope from mitigation.

A sudden, accidental pipe burst is often covered by homeowners insurance, while long-term leaks and neglect often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies. We explain scope and price before work starts, and you can call for your actual number.

If the water came from somewhere else

Not every plumbing failure is a burst line. A pipe that froze and thawed has its own page on frozen pipes, a tank failure is covered under water heater leaks, and a supply line behind a machine is on the appliance leak page. Safety steps before the crew arrives

Shut off the main water valve first. Stay out of rooms where water is near outlets, and do not use electrical devices while standing in water. If a ceiling is sagging with trapped water, keep people out from under it.

Lift small valuables and papers to a dry spot, photograph the damage and then call the line. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which guides professional drying, assumes the source has been stopped before drying starts.
