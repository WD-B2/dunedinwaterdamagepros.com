Water damage in Dunedin Isles

Dunedin Isles is a named residential area inside the City of Dunedin, in Pinellas County. We could not find a city map of its outline, so we describe it by name and do not claim exact boundaries. Real estate sources list it with a Dunedin ZIP code and use it for both single-family streets and townhouse communities.

The line answers around the clock. If water has reached floors, walls or a ceiling in a Dunedin Isles home, the sections below explain what to do first and how restoration works.

What real estate data says about the homes

A Homes.com neighborhood profile counts about 635 homes in Dunedin Isles, with a median year built of 1954 and large median lot sizes. It lists both single-family homes and townhouses. That is a real estate website's summary, not a city survey, so treat the numbers as approximate.

Housing age is useful on a call, but it does not tell us how much water damage an area has. We do not claim older homes leak more. What it does mean is that plumbing, roofs and finishes may have been repaired or replaced at different times, so tell the dispatcher what you know.

Single-family homes versus townhouses

In a single-family home, water usually stays within your walls and floors, and you control the shutoff. In a townhouse, water can move through shared walls and floors, and an association may control parts of the building. Tell the crew which one you have.

If the leak started next door, photograph what reached your unit and tell your association or manager. The wall and drywall page explains how wet walls are handled, and hardwood floors and carpet have their own pages.

If water is still spreading, before we arrive

Do what is safe, and leave the rest to the crew.

Stop the source

Close the shutoff valve at the fixture or the main water valve if you can reach it safely.

Cut power only if it is safe

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is on dry ground and you are not standing in water. For sparking or a gas smell, call the utility or 911 first.

Lift valuables and furniture

Move small furniture, papers and electronics to dry surfaces, and photograph the damage before you move things.

Keep people and pets out

Stay away from standing water that may be contaminated or near electrical equipment, then call the 24/7 line.

What shapes the cost of a Dunedin Isles job

No honest company can name a number before it sees the water. These factors set the scope.

  • Water categoryClean supply water costs less to handle than storm water or contaminated water.
  • Area and materialsRooms, square footage and whether carpet, wood or drywall is wet change the scope.
  • Drying daysEquipment runs until readings show dry, so humidity and materials affect the days.
  • Rebuild scopeRemoved materials add repair work after drying, which is separate from mitigation.

Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies. We explain scope and price before work starts.

Extraction and drying, step by step

The crew finds the source, removes standing water, takes out materials that cannot be saved and sets air movers and dehumidifiers. Readings are taken until the structure is dry, following the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference. The water extraction page covers the first hours, and the structural drying page covers the equipment.

Gulf coast humidity slows drying, so dehumidification is part of every plan. If a dark spot appears after a leak, the mold remediation page explains the next step.

Insurance documentation and permits

Photograph the damage and the source before cleanup, and keep a list of what was wet. Moisture readings from the crew support the record. Your policy and the cause of the loss decide coverage, so ask your insurer. We do not say how an insurer will respond.

For repairs beyond drying, ask the City of Dunedin Permits and Inspections office about permits. The city says its FEMA 50 percent rule applies only in a mapped Special Flood Hazard Area.
