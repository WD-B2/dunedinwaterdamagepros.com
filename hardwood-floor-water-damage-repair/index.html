What water does to wood floors

Wood takes on water and swells. If water gets under the boards, the bottoms absorb more than the tops, and the boards cup, which means the edges rise higher than the center. If the top absorbs more, the board crowns. Gaps open when boards dry and shrink.

This page covers wood and laminate. Carpet and pad are on the carpet water damage page, and a leak under a concrete floor is on the slab leak page.

Signs of water damage in a wood floor

Look for cupping, crowning, darkened boards, white or black stains, lifting seams, a musty smell, and boards that feel soft. Engineered wood and laminate show swelling at the edges.

Even if the floor looks fine, water may be under it. Moisture readings in the floor and the subfloor tell the real story.

Wood floor types and water

Each type behaves differently.

Solid hardwood

Can often be dried, though cupping may need sanding after.

Engineered wood

Layers can separate if soaked.

Laminate

Swells at edges and often needs replacement.

Bamboo

Reacts like wood and needs prompt drying.

Laminate, engineered wood and glued floors

Not every wood-look floor behaves the same way. Laminate has a fiberboard core that swells permanently when it gets wet. Engineered wood has a thin wood top over layers of plywood, and it can delaminate if soaked. Floors glued to a slab can trap water underneath, which drying mats cannot reach.

If you know the floor type and how it was installed, tell the dispatcher. If you do not know, a flooring professional can identify it before decisions are made about drying or removal.

Subfloor and moisture under the boards

The subfloor under hardwood is plywood or concrete, and both can hold water long after the surface dries. A meter that reads only the board face can miss it. Moisture readings in the subfloor, taken in several places, show whether the floor is ready to be sanded or refinished.

Refinishing too soon locks water into the wood, which can cause cupping to return. That is why measured dry readings come before any sanding and why the next steps are decided after drying, not before.

How wood floors are dried

Crews extract water, take readings and may use drying mats, which pull moisture out of boards from the surface. Dehumidifiers lower humidity so the boards release water. Sometimes boards are removed to dry the subfloor.

The goal is to dry the floor to a reading close to normal for your home. The timing matters, since wood left wet can stain and grow mold. See the structural drying page.

Safety steps while you wait

Do not walk on floors that are lifting or cupping, and do not use heat guns or fans aimed at the boards, which can crack them. Stay off wet floors near electrical outlets.

The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard guides drying of wet flooring, and crews use moisture meters to decide when boards are safe to keep.
