Appliance leaks start at hoses and valves

Most appliance water damage comes from a short list of parts. Washing machine supply hoses, dishwasher inlet valves, refrigerator ice maker lines and drain connections fail more often than the appliances themselves. A hose can give way while no one is home, which lets water run for hours.

This page covers the appliance as the source. If the water reached cabinets and a kitchen floor, the kitchen water damage page covers that damage, and a failed tank heater is on the water heater leak page.

When an appliance leak is an emergency

Water spreading from a washer or dishwasher is an emergency if it has reached flooring or the walls. Look for a wet floor beyond the appliance, a damp wall behind it or water dripping from a ceiling below.

If the leak is above a lower floor, in a condo or in a townhome, water may be reaching a neighbor. Tell the neighbor or property manager right away, and call this line, which is open 24/7.

Common appliance leak sources

Each appliance has its own weak points.

Washing machine

Supply hoses, drain hose connections and overflow from a clogged drain.

Dishwasher

Door gasket, inlet valve and drain hose clamp.

Refrigerator

Ice maker supply line and defrost drain.

Water softener and filters

Fittings, resin tank connections and drain lines.

Hidden damage behind and under appliances

The wet spot you can see is often smaller than the wet area you cannot. Water from a washer or dishwasher runs under the machine, into the cabinet toe kick and along the base of the wall. It can travel under vinyl and laminate planks where only a moisture meter will find it.

After a leak, ask the crew to check the wall behind the appliance, the cabinet base next to it and the floor several feet away. Slow, hidden moisture is how a small leak becomes a mold problem a few weeks later, which is why the readings matter more than the surface.

What to do while you wait

Close the appliance's shutoff valve. For a washer, there are two valves on the wall box. If you cannot reach one, close the main. Unplug the appliance only if the floor and outlet are dry.

Pull the appliance away from the wall if it is safe to move, and lift anything off the floor. Mop only small amounts and leave deeper water to extraction. Take photos of the appliance, the hose and the wet areas.

Related leaks from the same kitchen and utility room

A dishwasher or washer line fails the same way a plumbing line does, so the burst pipe page explains what a full pressure leak does. Water that slipped behind cabinets is a wall and drywall job, and the drying that follows is explained under structural drying.

Standards behind this page

Industry practice for water damage follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sorts water into categories by contamination. Supply line water starts clean but can degrade as it sits in carpet and pad, which is why the standard ties the category to time and conditions, not only to the source.
