Why air conditioners leak water in Florida

An air conditioner pulls moisture out of humid air and sends it down a condensate drain line. In Florida's humid climate, that line carries a lot of water, and it can clog with algae and debris. When it does, the drain pan fills and the water overflows into the ceiling, attic or closet.

A leaking air handler can wet drywall for hours before anyone sees a stain. The HVAC technician fixes the equipment. The restoration crew handles the wet building materials.

Spot the signs early

Signs include a ceiling stain near a vent or the air handler, water in the drain pan, a musty smell, a drip from a vent, or warped baseboards near the unit. Condensate leaks often leave rust-colored stains.

If the leak is from an attic air handler, the stain may appear far from the unit. Insulation holds the water and spreads it across the ceiling.

What to do when the AC is leaking

Follow this order.

1

Turn the AC off

Switch it off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate.

2

Check the pan and line

If you can reach it safely, look for water in the pan. A technician clears a clogged line.

3

Protect the floor

Place towels and a bucket, and move items from under any drip.

Then call a licensed HVAC technician for the equipment and call this line for water damage. They are two different jobs.

When it is urgent

This page is not classed as an emergency service by default, because many AC leaks are slow. But if the ceiling is sagging, dripping steadily or the stain is growing, call the line any time. It is open 24/7.

Standing water in an attic can soak insulation and ceiling drywall in a single weekend if the AC runs continuously.

When a damp ceiling turns into a mold question

Moisture that stays in drywall and insulation for days gives mold a place to start. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and an AC leak that runs overnight, or all weekend, can pass that window before anyone notices.

If you see dark spotting on the ceiling or smell mustiness near a vent, treat it as a moisture problem first. The leak has to be fixed, the wet material dried or removed, and then any mold handled to the S520 standard. Health questions belong to a physician.

Where AC water ends up

Condensate water rarely stays at the air handler. It runs into ceilings, which the ceiling water damage page explains, into walls, covered on the wall and drywall page, and into insulation and framing that need structural drying. A dark spot that returns after drying is explained on the mold remediation page.

Pinellas County summers are humid, and air conditioners run for months. That puts a steady load on condensate lines in homes from the Historic Southside to Curlew.
