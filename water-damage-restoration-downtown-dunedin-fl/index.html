Water damage in Downtown Dunedin

The City of Dunedin describes its Historic Downtown as a place for shops, dining and waterfront parks, and calls Dunedin the Best Little Walking Town in America. For a water damage call, that mix matters. A burst line in a second-floor condo, a roof leak over a restaurant and a soaked bungalow floor all start the same way but end very differently.

We do not publish exact boundaries for downtown, because the sources describe it as the area around Main Street and not as a mapped district. If your property is near Main Street, the waterfront or the Pinellas Trail, this page is written for you.

Three kinds of properties, three kinds of plans

Real estate guides describe downtown housing as a mix of bungalows and single-family homes, condos and townhomes near Main Street and the water, and larger waterfront homes. Add storefronts and offices, and a restoration plan has to fit the building.

For a house, the work usually starts with extraction and moves room by room. For a condo, the water often crosses a floor or a wall, so the crew needs to know who is below and above you. For a business, the questions are about stock, equipment and how soon the space can reopen.

If you own or run a storefront

A leak above a shop or restaurant can damage ceilings, inventory and equipment at once. Photograph the damage, move stock out of the water if you can do it safely and call. The commercial water damage page explains how business losses are planned, and the ceiling water damage page covers stained and sagging ceilings.

Tell the dispatcher whether you own or lease the space and whether neighboring tenants are affected. Property managers should have the building address, an on-site contact and the location of the main shutoff ready.

If water is still spreading, before we arrive

Do what is safe, and leave the rest to the crew.

Stop the source

Close the shutoff valve at the fixture or the main water valve if you can reach it safely.

Cut power only if it is safe

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is on dry ground and you are not standing in water. For sparking or a gas smell, call the utility or 911 first.

Lift valuables and furniture

Move small furniture, papers and electronics to dry surfaces, and photograph the damage before you move things.

Keep people and pets out

Stay away from standing water that may be contaminated or near electrical equipment, then call the 24/7 line.

What the city's own notices show about downtown life

City bulletins show how busy the core is. In May 2025 the city announced closures of Main Street between Broadway and Highland Avenue and of part of Douglas Avenue for an event. The Pinellas Trail and Edgewater Drive run through the same area.

Closures and event traffic can slow access, so tell the dispatcher about any road closure or loading restriction near your address. That helps plan how crews and equipment reach the building.

What shapes the cost of a downtown job

No honest company can name a number before it sees the water. These factors set the scope.

  • Water categoryClean supply water costs less to handle than rain intrusion or contaminated water.
  • Building typeA house, a condo and a storefront have different materials, access and shared walls.
  • Drying daysEquipment runs until readings show dry, and humidity affects how long that takes.
  • Rebuild scopeRemoved drywall, flooring and fixtures add repair work after drying, which is separate from mitigation.

Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies. We explain scope and price before work starts.

Older and historic buildings

Downtown is Dunedin's historic core, and many buildings there are older. If yours is, mention it on the call. Wet plaster, old finishes and aged wiring call for care, and a crew should check them before anything is removed.

If you are unsure whether a repair needs a permit, ask the City of Dunedin Permits and Inspections office. The structural drying page explains how a building is dried and measured.

Documenting the loss

Photos of the damage and the source, a list of what was wet and the moisture readings from the crew make a clear record. For a business, add notes on stock and equipment. Keep every receipt. Coverage depends on your policy and the cause of the damage, so ask your insurer what it needs.

We do not say how an insurer will respond. We explain scope and price before work starts, and we give you the readings. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard is the reference for how drying is measured, and you can ask any company to show readings.
