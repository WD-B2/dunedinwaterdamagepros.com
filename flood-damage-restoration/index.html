What flood damage cleanup covers

Flood damage restoration deals with water that enters a building from outside or rises from the ground. That includes surface flooding, storm surge, overflowing drainage and groundwater that pushes through a slab. The water is usually not clean, which changes how crews work and what can be saved.

This page covers rising and outside water. A plain roof leak is handled on the ceiling water damage page, and storm damage through the building shell is on the storm water damage page.

When floodwater is an emergency

Floodwater is an emergency from the first inch. It can carry bacteria, chemicals and debris, and it soaks into drywall, insulation and carpet quickly. It also hides electrical hazards.

Dunedin sits on St. Joseph Sound, and the City of Dunedin explains that its floodplain rules depend on the FEMA flood zone for each property. After the 2024 storm season the city began assessing damaged structures under FEMA requirements. If your home took on water, check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask the city building department what applies.

If floodwater is in your home

Safety comes before cleanup.

Stay out of the water

If power is on, assume the water can be energized. Do not wade through it to reach the panel.

Call the utility or 911 first for electrical or gas risk

A sparking outlet, a gas smell or a submerged panel is a utility and emergency call before it is a cleanup call.

Protect yourself

Wear boots and gloves if you must enter. Avoid contact with floodwater and wash after any exposure.

Document before moving things

Photograph water lines on walls and damaged contents for your insurer.

Water categories and what they mean

Restoration standards sort water into three categories. The category sets the safety steps.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat happens
Category 1Clean supply water, such as a broken lineExtraction and drying. Materials are often saved.
Category 2Gray water, such as appliance discharge or some storm intrusionExtraction, cleaning and a closer look at porous materials.
Category 3Sewage, rising floodwater, water from outsideContainment, removal of porous materials, disinfection and drying.

Rising floodwater from outside is generally treated as Category 3. The published standard ANSI/IICRC S500 describes these categories, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

What cleanup looks like

Crews start with safety, then pump and extract the water. Porous materials that sat in contaminated water, such as carpet pad, insulation and soaked drywall, are often removed and discarded rather than dried. Hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected.

Drying follows with air movers, dehumidifiers and moisture meters. If sewage is involved, the sewage backup cleanup page covers the added safety steps. Drying is described on the structural drying page.

What changes the price of flood cleanup

Flood jobs vary more than most, so the factors matter.

  • Water categoryCategory 3 water adds containment, protective gear and disposal.
  • Depth and durationDeeper water that sat longer reaches more materials.
  • Square footageFloor area sets extraction and equipment counts.
  • Materials removedPad, insulation and drywall that cannot be saved are removed and later replaced.
  • Drying daysEquipment time is set by measured moisture readings.

Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowners insurance and needs separate flood coverage. Sudden water from a burst pipe is treated differently. Check your policy and ask your insurer before assuming either way. We explain scope and price before work starts.
