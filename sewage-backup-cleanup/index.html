Drains gurgling and water coming up in the tub or floor drain?

That is how a sewage backup often starts. Toilets burble, a shower drain bubbles, or dirty water rises in a floor drain. It means wastewater is flowing the wrong way, and everything it touches becomes contaminated.

The restoration term for this is sewage backup cleanup, also called Category 3 water damage. It is not a mop-and-bleach job.

Sewage backup is an emergency

Sewage can carry bacteria and viruses. The danger is exposure, so the first rule is to keep people and pets out of the affected rooms. Do not use fans to dry it yourself, and do not wipe it up with household tools.

The line is open 24/7, so call at any hour. Crews wear protective equipment, contain the area and remove what cannot be cleaned.

Sewage backup: what to do first

Safety steps before help arrives.

Stop using water

Do not flush toilets or run sinks, showers or the washer. More water adds to the backup.

Stay out and keep others out

Children and pets should not enter the room. Avoid contact with the water.

Mind the power

If outlets or the panel are near the water, do not step in. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical risk before you call us.

Find the cause

A clog in the home's line, or a problem in the city main, causes backups. A plumber can tell which.

What not to do after a sewage backup

Do not run fans to push the smell away, because air movement can spread contamination to clean rooms. Do not wash sewage down a drain, mop it with household cleaners or carry soaked items through the house. Each of those spreads the problem.

Do not eat food that touched the water, and wash your hands after any contact. Keep a list of what was in the affected room, along with photos, since your insurer will ask. Health questions after exposure belong to a physician.

What happens to materials in a sewage backup

Porous materials absorb contamination and are usually removed.

MaterialUsual resultWhy
Carpet and padRemoved and discardedContaminants soak into fibers.
DrywallCut out if wettedPorous and hard to disinfect.
Hard floors and tileCleaned and disinfectedNon-porous surfaces can be cleaned.
InsulationRemovedAbsorbs and holds contamination.

How crews clean up sewage

Crews set up containment, extract the water, remove porous materials, and clean and disinfect remaining surfaces. Then the structure is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Industry practice follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

If the backup came with floodwater from a storm, the flood damage cleanup page covers the larger picture. Drying is explained on the structural drying page.
