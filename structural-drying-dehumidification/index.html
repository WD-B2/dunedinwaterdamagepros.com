Wall still damp days after the leak was fixed?

Fixing the leak stops the water. It does not dry the drywall, framing, flooring and insulation that got wet. A wall that feels cool, a floor that smells musty or paint that bubbles weeks later all point to moisture that is still inside.

Structural drying and dehumidification is the trade name for the process that fixes this. It uses measurement to find hidden moisture and equipment to remove it.

Water mitigation explained

Water mitigation means the steps that stop water damage from growing. That includes stopping the source, extracting water and drying the structure. Drying is the part that takes days and the part that is easiest to get wrong without measurement.

The industry standard is ANSI/IICRC S500. It describes how water loss classes and drying goals are set, and you can ask any company if its crews work to it.

How structural drying works

It follows a set pattern.

1

Moisture mapping

Meters and thermal cameras find wet areas, which are marked and recorded.

2

Equipment setup

Air movers push air across wet surfaces, and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air.

3

Daily readings

Crews return to take readings and move equipment until the structure meets dry goals.

Drying ends when readings match dry reference values, not when a set number of days passes.

What goes wrong when drying is rushed

A surface that feels dry can still be holding water. Drywall can look fine while the stud cavity behind it stays wet, and a floor can feel dry while the subfloor under it does not. If the equipment is removed too soon, the moisture settles back and mold becomes a risk.

That is why dry is defined by readings taken in the same places on each visit. If a crew cannot show you the numbers, ask for them. Documented readings also help with an insurance claim.

LGR dehumidifierRemoves moisture from air at low humidityWhole-room drying.
Desiccant dehumidifierDries air in cooler or very damp conditionsSpecialized jobs.

How long drying takes

Drying often takes several days, depending on the materials, the amount of water and how humid the air is. Florida's humidity makes the work harder, because the air already holds moisture. That is why dehumidifiers run with the air movers and why doors and windows stay closed.

A job's duration is decided by readings. A crew that quotes a fixed number of days before measuring is guessing.

What drives structural drying costs

Drying is priced by equipment and time.

  • Class of water lossLarger wet areas and denser materials need more equipment.
  • Square footageRooms and walls affected.
  • MaterialsHardwood and concrete dry slowly.
  • Equipment daysSet by readings.
  • Monitoring visitsDaily checks until dry.

Where drying fits in the rest of the job

Drying starts after the water is removed, as the water extraction page explains. It protects walls covered on the wall and drywall page and floors covered on the hardwood floor page, and it is the main defense against the dark spotting described on the mold remediation page.

Safety steps while drying runs

Keep cords off wet floors, do not unplug or move equipment, and keep children and pets away from running machines. Tell the crew if anyone has asthma or allergies.

Equipment runs for days. Ask the crew what noise and power to expect so you are not surprised.

Drying in a humid Dunedin climate

Dunedin's Gulf coast humidity means the air is rarely dry. That is a challenge for drying and a reason to use dehumidification, not just fans.

If your home has central air, keep it running as the crew advises, and keep windows closed so outside humid air does not add to the load.
