What crawl space water damage looks like

A crawl space is the shallow, unfinished area under a raised floor. When water gets in, it soaks insulation, floor joists, the underside of the subfloor and any ductwork or plumbing running through it. The problem is easy to miss, because no one lives there and the damage shows up first as a musty smell, a soft floor or humidity inside.

This page applies to homes with a raised foundation. If your home is built on a slab, a leak under the floor is a different problem, covered on the slab leak page.

How water gets into a crawl space

Common sources are plumbing leaks, a clogged or overflowing condensate line, drainage that runs toward the house and storm water that pools around vents. Humid air that settles on cool surfaces is a source of moisture too.

In Pinellas County, a high water table and heavy rain can leave low spots wet for days. Whether a crawl space is a concern for your home depends on how the house was built, so ask a contractor who can see it.

Signs the crawl space has a moisture problem

Look for these clues from inside and outside.

Musty odor

A smell that rises through the floor vents.

Soft or bouncy floor

Joists or subfloor that stayed wet can soften.

Sagging insulation

Wet batts drop away from the floor above.

Standing water or damp soil

Visible water at the access hatch.

How a crawl space is dried

The work has to be done in a tight space.

1

Make it safe

Check power and plumbing, and wear protective gear.

2

Remove water and wet insulation

Extract standing water and take out batts that cannot dry.

3

Dry and monitor

Use air movers and a dehumidifier, and take moisture readings.

The goal is to return wood to a safe moisture reading, not to make the space look dry.

Why crawl space moisture matters for the rooms above

Air moves between a crawl space and the living area through floor gaps, plumbing penetrations and duct leaks. Damp air below carries that moisture and any odor upward, which is why a wet crawl space can raise indoor humidity and leave a musty smell in closets and cabinets.

Wood framing that stays damp also loses strength over time. A contractor can check joists and the subfloor with a moisture meter before deciding what needs drying and what needs replacement. Do that before covering anything with new insulation or a vapor barrier.

How crawl space water reaches the house above

Moisture below the floor rises into the living space. Wet subfloors affect the hardwood floor and carpet pages, standing water is removed as described under water extraction, and the structural drying page explains how framing is dried.

Safety steps before you enter

Do not enter a crawl space with standing water, strong odors or exposed wiring. Wear a respirator, gloves and eye protection if you must look inside, and never go in alone.
