Dark spots in the corner of the shower, a closet wall or a ceiling after a leak?

That is how many people first notice mold. Spots appear where a leak left the wall damp, such as under a sink, behind a toilet, in a closet that touches a wet wall or on a ceiling below an air handler. The restoration term for fixing it is mold remediation.

Mold is a moisture problem first. The leak has to be stopped and the wet material dried before any mold work makes sense.

What mold needs to grow

Mold needs moisture, a food source such as drywall paper, wood or dust, and time. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency says that if wet materials are dried within 24 to 48 hours, mold generally will not grow. In humid Florida, that window is hard to meet after a leak, which is why quick drying matters.

Without moisture, mold stops growing. That is why cleaning a stain without fixing the water source tends to fail.

Testing and inspection

Callers often ask for an inspection or independent testing. A mold inspection finds the visible mold and the moisture behind it. Testing can include air or surface samples. Independent clearance testing, done by someone who is not doing the removal, is used after remediation to confirm the area is ready.

Florida licenses individuals as mold assessors and mold remediators under Chapter 468, Part XVI of the Florida Statutes, and the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation administers it. It does not license businesses. Ask whether the person doing the work holds the right state license.

Mold after a plumbing leak, a roof leak or an air handler

Callers most often describe mold that follows a specific leak. An upstairs toilet that wet a ceiling below, a leak under a bathroom sink, a roof leak into a closet or a clogged AC condensate line are common starting points. The mold shows up near the water, usually on the ceiling or the lower part of a wall.

Tell us what leak came first. That tells the crew where to look for hidden moisture, and which related page applies, such as ceiling water damage or AC leak damage.

How mold remediation works

Industry practice follows ANSI/IICRC S520, the published standard for mold remediation. You can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

1

Fix the moisture

The leak is repaired and wet materials are dried.

2

Contain

Plastic barriers and negative air machines keep spores from spreading.

3

Remove and clean

Porous materials with mold are removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned with HEPA vacuums and damp wiping.

After removal, materials are dried and the area is checked. No honest company promises mold will never return, since that depends on moisture control.

Mold and selling or financing a home

A buyer's lender or an inspector may ask for mold to be addressed before a sale or a loan closes. In that case, documentation matters: the moisture source repair, the remediation scope and a clearance test from an independent assessor.

We cannot promise how a lender or buyer will respond. Ask the lender what documentation it requires, and ask a real estate professional about disclosure.

What drives mold remediation costs

Mold work varies a lot.

  • Area affectedLarger area, larger scope.
  • MaterialsDrywall removal versus surface cleaning.
  • ContainmentBarriers and air filtration add to the job.
  • TestingInspection and clearance testing are separate costs.
  • Moisture repairThe plumber or roofer fixes the source.

Insurance may cover mold that results from a covered water loss, and policies often limit mold coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer. We explain scope and price before work starts.

Find the water first

Mold follows moisture, so the source matters. A leaking line is covered on the burst pipe page, and wet drywall that was never dried is explained on the wall and drywall page. The structural drying page explains how a building is dried and measured.

When to call and what not to do

Do not disturb large areas of mold, do not paint over it and do not mix bleach with ammonia. Call a professional for areas larger than about 10 square feet, or after sewage or flood water.
